# analyze-storytelling: how the account builds a story

In plain words: look at the **structure** of each video. How does a few
sentences of speech become a story that people follow and believe? The goal is
to find the story shapes the account **repeats**.

## Step 0: read the playbook first (always)
Read `.claude/skills/instagram-playbook/SKILL.md`, mainly "specific beats
vague", promises in parts, and formats.

## Step 1: gather the transcripts
- **Text pasted in the chat:** use it directly.
- **A folder:** read every transcript file (`.txt`), one file per video.
- **Skip thin transcripts** (fewer than 15 words) and say how many you skipped.

If it is not clear where the transcripts are, ask only that.

## Step 2: take each story apart
For each video, find and **quote the exact words**:
- **Shape of the story:** problem, turning point, solution? Before and after? A
  day by day journey? A claim followed by proof?
- **Characters:** who appears (the speaker, a guide, a local person, a child,
  the viewer as "you")? What is each one there for?
- **Concrete examples:** specific scenes that make the idea real.
- **Promise and payoff:** what is promised early, and is it delivered?
- **Emotional moments:** where does the feeling go up or down (fear, relief,
  humor, pride)?
- **Specific or vague:** how much is concrete detail and how much is general
  talk?

## Step 3: find the patterns
- Which **story shapes** come back most often?
- Is there a **signature script** (for example: doubt, then a trip with a named
  guide, then a wider meaning)?
- How are **named people** and the viewer ("you") used?
- How does it balance **personal story** and **message**?
- Where is it strongest, and where does it slip into vague language?

## Step 4: check your work
Confirm that every quote appears word for word in a transcript.

## What to deliver
Save as `analysis-storytelling.md` in the account's workspace folder, with:

1. **Evidence:** transcripts used and skipped.
2. **The storytelling style in short** (2 to 4 lines).
3. **Video by video:** story shape, key moments and payoff, with quotes.
4. **Patterns:** the repeated script shapes and how characters are used.
5. **Specific or vague:** where it is concrete and where it is generic.
6. **Reusable script shapes:** 1 to 3 outlines taken from the account itself.

Always based on real quotes. No storytelling theory without evidence.
